03

Scoring system

Public federal data, built the way commercial property models are weighted

What goes into the score
  • Inland floodingNRI riverine and inland flood risk: flooding frequency from rivers, streams and heavy rain times the value exposed, scored 0 to 100 nationally. Pennsylvania's main property peril.
  • Winter weatherNRI winter weather risk: snow, ice and freezing loss. Most of the state scores high; it drives roof, ice-dam and frozen-pipe claims.
  • Strong windNRI strong wind risk: damaging thunderstorm and straight-line wind loss to buildings.
  • ResilienceNRI community resilience: local capacity to recover, used as a small adjustment.
  • AvailabilityCarrier models (flood zone determinations, elevation) are property-level and not published by ZIP. This composite uses public federal data on the same ingredients.

Tier rules
  • ExtremeInland flood score 96+, or a score of 90+ with 70%+ of the ZIP's residents in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for flooding
  • HighInland flood score 90+, or a score of 80+ with 40%+ of residents in high flood tracts
  • ModerateInland flood score 65+, 30%+ of residents in high flood tracts, or any single tract scoring 97+
  • LowEverything else. Winter weather and wind raise the score but do not set the tier, because nearly every ZIP carries them.
Reference points
  • River ZIPsCroydon 19021 scores 85.3 (Extreme); Bridgeport 19405 scores 74.2 (High); Harrisburg 17110 scores 75.9 (High)
  • Your home marketSpringfield 19064 scores 39.4 (Low); Media 19063 scores 44.1 (Low); Broomall 19008 scores 47.0 (Low). Large ZIPs blend creekside streets with higher ground, so a Low ZIP can still hold flood-zone homes.

Flood and winter as a cross-sell

Homeowners policies exclude flood; winter claims depend on the roof

Why it matters here
  • Coverage gapStandard homeowners policies do not cover flood. In High and Extreme ZIPs a flood quote belongs in the first conversation, not the renewal.
  • Rain, not riversHeavy rain floods streets and basements well outside mapped flood zones. Sewer and drain backup coverage is a low-cost add-on worth offering everywhere, including Delaware County.
  • Winter58% of Pennsylvanians live in tracts rated Relatively High or Very High for winter weather; Springfield 19064 scores 86 of 100. Roof age and water-damage coverage are the questions to ask at quote time.
  • Second homes78% of homes in Pocono Pines 18350 were vacant at survey time, most of them seasonal. Pocono Home leads often mean a second home: ask how the home is used, and quote vacant-home and flood coverage to match.
